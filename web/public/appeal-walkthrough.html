Evidence can lead to a warning.
New context can change it.

A real Meta request, independent local ML, an explained confirmation and a report-scoped appeal. This sequence was performed in the browser with a separate fictional account, preserving all thirteen previous reports.

For this scripted fictional appeal, permission to mirror a portfolio is assumed as new context. The prototype does not verify Instagram account ownership. Similarity is evidence of content reuse, not deceptive intent.

Step 01

Real Meta and independent ML

The live Meta response reports 90% visual reuse; the new text model reports 95.7%. Four photo regions match and two contact lines are masked. Unreadable suspect OCR fields were corrected before submission.

Step 02

An explained moderator decision

The fictional report is explicitly confirmed, with a written reason. The profile warning survives reloading.

Step 03

A way to challenge the decision

The scoped appeal introduces permission context. The warning remains while the appeal awaits review.

Step 04

Two decisions, one private history

The moderator explicitly overturns the example. Both reasons and the appeal remain visible after a server restart.

Step 05

The affected party can see the outcome

Refresh report shows the saved moderator response and that this report no longer adds a warning.

Step 06

A profile without this warning

The final rejected report does not add an impersonation warning.
