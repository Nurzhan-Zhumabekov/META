MirrorCheck gives familiar feeds a clearer signal. Our new landing page introduces the product with a moving gallery and liquid glass navigation. The goal is simple: turn suspected profile copies into evidence, while keeping the decision human.

From the suspected copy, I choose Report account, then Pretending to be someone else. I upload screenshots of both profiles. Paddle O C R extracts the visible names, biographies and counts. Privacy comes before the model request. Presidio detects the example contact details locally, and the server masks those regions in the images. I can inspect the exact processed previews. The moderator later sees the same processed evidence that was prepared for Meta AI.

Meta Muse Spark compares the processed screenshots through the backend. This is a real, fresh response from the recorded case. It returns visible similarities, differences, and a review recommendation. The interface separates Meta's visual score, the combined score, text reuse M L, and profile spam patterns. These are signals, not identity probabilities.

The report remains under review until a moderator acts. In the protected review queue, I inspect the processed images, matching photo regions, the model's observations, and its limitations. Confirmation is a deliberate human action. Here, the moderator confirms this fictional copied account.

The decision is saved on the server. I return to the reported profile and reload the page. The impersonation warning remains visible. An analysis score alone never creates this label; the moderator's saved decision does.

The opposite outcome is possible too. This earlier fictional report concerns an ordinary profile. The moderator dismissed it, and the account has no warning. Shared stock photographs can create visual overlap without establishing impersonation.

MirrorCheck is a working local prototype. Its text model uses synthetic training pairs, and performance on real account copies still needs independent evaluation. Human review remains essential.
